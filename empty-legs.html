The basics

What are empty-leg flights?

Private jets sometimes need to fly without passengers to reposition for their next journey. These flights are called empty legs. Operators may offer them at reduced prices, creating opportunities to experience private aviation for less.

How an empty leg happens

A client charters a jet from London to Nice. The aircraft is based in London and its next job is in Geneva, so after dropping off its passengers it flies on — empty. That Nice-to-Geneva flight is an empty leg. The operator has to fly it anyway, so some choose to offer it at a lower price rather than fly with no one aboard.

What to expect

  • Usually one-way. The route follows where the aircraft needs to go, so your return needs separate planning.
  • Whole-aircraft pricing. You pay for the jet, not a seat. Splitting the cost with friends or family is how it can get closer to airline-style prices — but that is your arrangement, not something JetDrop offers.
  • Flexible by necessity. Departure airports, dates and times are set by the operator’s schedule.
  • Subject to change. If the operator’s plans change, an empty leg can be rescheduled or cancelled, sometimes at short notice.

Questions worth asking the provider

  • Is this flight still available, and is the price all-inclusive (taxes, handling, landing fees)?
  • Who is the operating carrier, and what are the cancellation and rescheduling terms?
  • What happens if the aircraft’s schedule changes?
  • What are the luggage and passenger limits?
