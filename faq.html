FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

What are empty-leg flights?

Empty legs are positioning flights a private jet has to make without passengers — for example, flying back to its home base after a charter. Operators may offer them at a reduced price. See our guide.

Why can they be discounted?

The aircraft is going to fly the route regardless, so an operator may prefer to recover some of the cost. There is no guarantee any particular flight is discounted; always compare against the operator’s standard pricing.

Are prices per passenger or per aircraft?

Prices on JetDrop are for the whole aircraft. Where we show a per-person figure it is only an illustration of splitting the full price, and individual seats are not sold.

Can flights be cancelled?

Yes. Empty legs depend on the operator’s schedule and can change or be cancelled. Prices and availability can change without notice, which is why you should confirm with the provider before making plans.

Are return flights included?

Usually not. Empty legs are normally one-way. Ask the provider whether a return is possible and at what cost.

How do bookings work?

You don’t book with JetDrop. Click through to the original operator or authorized booking provider, confirm availability with them, and complete your booking and payment with that provider.

How do alerts work?

You choose departure airports, a nearby-airport radius, destinations and a maximum whole-aircraft budget. When a matching listing appears we’ll email you. Alerts are in demonstration mode until the backend is connected, and a match is never guaranteed.

What does membership include?

Free Explorer (€0) includes browsing, a weekly digest and basic airport search. JetDrop Plus (€9.99/month, not yet available) adds instant alerts, multiple saved searches, nearby-airport matching, price-drop notifications and advanced filters. Flights are never included.
